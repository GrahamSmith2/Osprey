# Osprey USV — Rudder Sizing & Heading Control: Results Summary

Every number below comes from the Python model and is regenerated by
`python -m osprey report` (tables in `python/report.md` alongside this file).
Every number is conditional on the assumptions in `../ASSUMPTIONS.md`, and the
important ones are named inline.

**Read `../README.md` first.** The hull derivatives underpinning all of this are
swept ±5× because the standard regressions are being used an order of magnitude
outside their validity domain (Fn 2.9–7.8 vs Fn < 0.3). Nothing here is a
prediction of the boat's trajectory.

**The race these results describe** (team decisions, September 2026): new rudder
45 mm chord × 5 in submerged; **50 mph cruise from a standing start**; two marks
0.25 mile apart rounded at 25 m, 2 miles; the autopilot `oval_autopilot.lua`
steering with the **motors below 10 mph and the rudder above**. Propulsion is
**assumed** upgraded so the loaded boat tops out at 55 mph — the prop as logged
tops out at 28.8 mph (§6).

An earlier version of this summary came from the MATLAB model at 8 m/s on a
placeholder circle. What changed, and what was wrong in it, is in §9.

---

## 1. Headline: the rudder should be sized on SPAN, not area

Rudder authority is set by the product **A_r · CL_α**, not by area alone. Growing
area at fixed span means growing chord, which collapses the aspect ratio, and
`CL_α` falls almost as fast as `A_r` rises. Open-loop turn at 10° (the useful
limit), 10 m/s:

| blade | span | chord | area | AR | CL_α [1/rad] | A_r·CL_α | turn radius [LOA] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| as built | 76 mm (3 in) | 30 mm | 1.0× | 2.54 | 2.85 | 1.00× | 12.64 |
| 3× area, same span | 76 mm | 90 mm | 3.0× | 0.85 | 1.21 | 1.27× | 11.61 |
| **30× area, same span** | 76 mm | 900 mm | 30× | 0.08 | 0.13 | **1.32×** | 11.68 |
| 3.3× area via **span** | 251 mm | 30 mm | 3.3× | 8.38 | 4.55 | **5.26×** | 7.20 |
| 5 in × 30 mm | 127 mm | 30 mm | 1.7× | 4.23 | 3.69 | 2.16× | 9.53 |
| **5 in × 45 mm (chosen)** | 127 mm | 45 mm | 2.5× | 2.82 | 3.03 | **2.66×** | 8.87 |

**Thirty times the area at fixed span buys 32% more authority. Growing the span
instead buys 426% for a tenth of the area increase.**

## 2. The rudder the team chose: 45 mm × 5 in

The blade goes 2 in deeper on the same mount, and 50% wider in chord.

| | as built (30 mm × 3 in) | **chosen (45 mm × 5 in)** |
|---|---|---|
| wetted area | 3.54 in² | 8.86 in² |
| authority A·CL_α | 1.00× | **2.66×** |
| turn radius, 10°, 5 m/s | 13.3 LOA (28 m) | **8.9 LOA (19 m)** |
| 90° turn at 5 m/s | 9.7 s | 6.7 s |
| rudder drag, 10°, 10 m/s | 4.3% of hull | **11.0%** |
| cavitation ceiling | 44 mph | 44 mph |

**The trade in the chord.** At 5 in of span, going from 30 to 45 mm of chord
adds 23% authority (2.16× → 2.66×) for 50% more area, 51% more drag and 1.8× the
hinge moment. The depth is what buys most of the authority. The chord is still a
defensible choice — it is margin against the Munk moment (§4b, §5) — but it is
paid for in servo load (§3) and drag.

## 3. Servo requirement

Peak servo torque [kg·cm] from straight running, travel clamped at ventilation
onset (autopilot) / full mechanical travel to 35° (manual). AGFRC A81FHM HV
stall is **74 kg·cm**. "0.10c off" is a stock at 25% chord with the centre of
pressure 0.10 chord off nominal — the realistic balanced case.

| speed | stock at LE | 25% c, CoP 0.10c off | 25% c, CoP nominal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 mph | 37 / 47 | 15 / 19 | ~0 |
| **50 mph** | **103 / 132** | **41 / 53** | ~0 |
| 70 mph | 202 / 258 | 81 / 103 | ~0 |

**With the full travel available, the peak is past ventilation onset**, near
26°: ventilated lift keeps growing with angle. The autopilot clamps at onset,
so the first figure is the one that applies in autonomous running.

**In the simulated 50 mph race the servo never sees more than ~8 kg·cm** even
with the CoP offset, because in a turn the boat's own sideslip and yaw rate cut
the blade's angle of attack to a fraction of its deflection. The table is the
step-input worst case.

**Verdict: the servo is adequate at 50 mph only with the stock near 25% chord.**
With a leading-edge stock it is overloaded by 40% clamped. Fixes: rebalance the
stock, a 2:1 tiller arm, or a stronger servo. **Measure the stock position
(ASSUMPTIONS A10) and the tiller arm (A9) first.** The tiller arm ratio scales
every number here linearly and is assumed 1:1.

**The thesis's 66.8 kg·cm** (80 mph, drag × horn radius) against a correct hinge
moment for their blade (75 mm submerged) with a leading-edge stock: **64.6
clamped, 82.7 full travel**. The thesis number is about right for an autopilot
that clamps at 10°, and 20% short for full travel. Its lever (the servo horn) is
the wrong one; it lands close by coincidence. (An earlier version of this
summary said the thesis was conservative by 46%. That was wrong — §9.)

**Servo rate.** The datasheet no-load speed was not supplied. The model assumes
0.10 s/60° (600°/s). A stop-to-stop slew in 0.5 s needs 140°/s, so there is
margin at the assumed value — but confirm it: the rate limit caps achievable
derivative gain.

## 4. Gain schedule

Nomoto identification on the nonlinear model (5° step, below ventilation onset):

| rudder | K′ | T′ | K ∝ U, T ∝ 1/U holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| chosen 45 mm × 5 in | 0.653 (±11%) | 0.490 (±16%) | yes |
| as built 30 mm × 3 in | 0.509 (±10%) | 1.063 (±14%) | yes |

The scaling is the result that survives the ±5× hull-derivative uncertainty.

**The autopilot's yaw-rate loop works in moment units.** Its proportional gain
`Kp_N = wc·T′·L²·KN/K′` is **speed-invariant** — 76.6 N·m per rad/s for the new
blade — and the `u²` dependence sits in the conversion from moment to actuator
command, which uses measured speed. Consequences:

1. The same gain drives the motors below 10 mph and the rudder above, so the
   handover needs no retuning and the integrator carries across.
2. The script does its own gain scheduling; ArduPilot Rover's lack of native
   scheduling does not matter as long as the script drives the outputs.
3. **`Kp ∝ 1/U` is right for a single-loop heading→rudder PID**, not for this
   cascade, where the angle-domain gain goes as `1/U²` and the moment-domain
   gain is flat.
4. **`Kd` on heading is unusable.** A useful lead zero needs `Kd_ψ ≈ 3`; the
   1.5° heading noise differentiated at 50 Hz is 1.85 rad/s, so that gain would
   inject 5.6 rad/s of command noise. Damping comes from the inner rate loop on
   the gyro instead (463× cleaner). This is the concrete justification for the
   cascade over a single-loop PID.

## 4b. Monte Carlo robustness at 50 mph — the design does NOT close

60 Latin-hypercube draws over all 17 declared uncertainties, **the same draws
for both rudders**, the autopilot's constants fixed at the nominal design, the
full race from a standing start, 15 kt crosswind and 0.5 m/s current.
**Pass** = finished, middle-of-straight error ≤ 5 m, never within 5 m of a
mark. **Lost** = more than 150 m off the course line. **Timed out** = still on
the course at three times the race time.

| rudder | pass | lost | timed out | median straight-line max | median swing-wide at marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| as built 30 mm × 3 in | 23% | 40% | 23% | 4.0 m | 40 m |
| **chosen 45 mm × 5 in** | **48%** | **25%** | **13%** | 3.8 m | 40 m |

**The new blade halves the failures (63% → 38%) — and 38% is still far from
acceptable.** The nominal boat runs cleanly (§4d); these are boats whose hull
coefficients sit elsewhere in the ±5× range.

### The failures are real, and there are three kinds

Every kind was checked by replaying draws, because a dramatic result is more
often a scoring bug than physics (HANDOFF §7 — and one was, §9 item 7):

1. **Spin-out during the launch** (all the timeouts, ~13%). The boat spins
   1.4–3 s after the start, at 13–19 m/s, and circles on the spot. Every one
   has strong sway–yaw coupling (`Nv` 1.6–4.9× nominal).
2. **Cannot make the first rounding** (most of the lost). The rudder sits at
   the 10° clamp and the boat still turns at only ~5°/s, a ~230 m radius, and
   runs off the course. These draws are stiff in yaw (`Nr` up to 4.9×) or
   weak in sway (`Yv` ~0.3×).
3. **Turns against the rudder.** Rudder hard over to starboard, boat turning
   steadily to port: the destabilising Munk moment wins (§5).

Median parameters of the lost draws against all draws (chosen rudder): yaw
damping `Nr` 0.65 vs 0.99, sway damping `Yv` 0.49 vs 1.00, `Nv` 0.45 vs 1.01,
yaw inertia 1.17 vs 1.00. Rank correlation with the worst distance off the line:
**`Nv` −0.55**, `Nr` +0.31, sway added mass `Y_v̇` +0.26, `Iz` −0.24,
ventilation onset +0.21.

### Speed matters less than expected

Same draws, same boat, chosen rudder; only the commanded cruise changes:

| cruise | pass | lost | timed out |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 mph | 55% | 17% | 12% |
| 30 mph | 55% | 15% | 13% |
| 40 mph | 50% | 17% | 13% |
| 50 mph | 48% | 25% | 13% |

Going from 20 to 50 mph costs ~7 points of pass rate and ~8 of lost boats. The
launch spin-outs do not change with cruise speed at all — every launch is full
throttle. **Racing slower does not close the design; knowing the hull does.**

### What this means

1. **The chosen blade is the right direction** — it halves the failures and
   never saturates in the nominal race.
2. **The on-water hull tests are the critical path** (§7, tests 1–3). With
   `Nv`, `Nr`, `Yv` and `Y_v̇` unknown to ±5×, no controller tuned at nominal
   can be validated; the sim cannot substitute for the measurements.
3. **Soften the launch.** A throttle ramp or an acceleration limit at the start
   is the cheapest thing to try against the launch spin-outs, and the assumed
   propulsion (0 → 15 m/s in 1.5 s) makes the launch harsher than a real one.
   Not yet tested.
4. **Do not chase gains** until the hull is measured: the failures are
   authority and stability, not tuning.

## 4c. Failure modes

Chosen rudder, 50 mph, 15 kt crosswind and 0.5 m/s current, fault injected at
10 s with the boat settled mid-way up the first straight. Plant faults are not
reported to the controller; it sees only its sensors.

| fault | finishes | lost | heading change in 5 s | peak lat g | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | yes | no | 2° | 1.39 | baseline |
| **rudder jammed at 10°** | **no** | no | **269°** | 2.61 | spins on the spot |
| **rudder jammed centred** | **no** | **yes** | 20° | 0.31 | cannot turn at the mark |
| port motor out | yes | no | 4° | 0.89 | rudder trims out the asymmetry |
| ventilation latched | yes | no | 2° | 1.40 | barely registers |
| GPS lost for 5 s | no | no | 10° | 0.21 | **disarms at 10.5 s (Rule 21)** |

**Ventilation barely registers** — a direct payoff from clamping the rudder at
onset: the autopilot normally uses a few degrees, so a 50% authority loss means
it uses a few more. **One motor out** is survivable: the rudder trims the
asymmetric thrust and the boat finishes slower.

**GPS loss kills the boat within 500 ms, as Rule 21 requires.** The boat does
not lurch when GPS goes away — heading comes from the IMU — so the kill is safe
to arm on the first missed fix.

**Any rudder failure above 10 mph ends the race.** That is a direct
consequence of the steering split: above 10 mph the motors never steer. The
physics of the fix:

| speed [mph] | rudder jammed at 10° [N·m] | most the motors can do [N·m] | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 5 | 124 | +119 |
| 10 | 20 | 117 | +97 |
| 15 | 44 | 109 | +65 |
| 20 | 78 | 102 | +24 |
| 30 | 176 | 87 | −89 |
| 50 | 489 | 57 | −432 |

A jammed rudder's moment grows as `u²`; differential thrust barely changes with
speed. Below ~22 mph the motors win. **Recommended fault response: on a
persistent yaw-rate error with the rudder not saturated, cut speed below 10 mph
and steer on the motors.** A jammed rudder never reports saturation, so the
detector has to be the rate error. Not implemented.

## 4d. The full race at 50 mph

From a standing start, 2 miles, 25 m roundings:

| rudder | conditions | time | avg mph | straights: max off line | widest from mark | peak lat g | peak rudder |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chosen | calm | 166.3 s | 43.3 | 3.2 m | 59 m | 1.35 | 6.5° |
| chosen | 15 kt + 0.5 m/s current | 166.7 s | 43.2 | 3.4 m | 62 m | 1.39 | 7.4° |
| as built | calm | 166.2 s | 43.3 | 3.1 m | 60 m | 1.34 | 7.9° |
| as built | 15 kt + 0.5 m/s current | 166.3 s | 43.3 | 3.1 m | 62 m | 1.37 | 9.5° |

**At nominal parameters both blades run the race cleanly**, and nothing ever
reaches the ventilation clamp. The steering split works as specified: the
motors steer for the first 0.8 s of the launch, the rudder for the rest, never
both at once.

**The boat cannot hold a 25 m rounding at 50 mph.** The autopilot's yaw-rate cap
(0.6 rad/s, just inside the roll limit — §5) sets a ~37 m minimum radius at
that speed, so the boat swings up to 34 m wide of the arc. It never cuts inside
a mark (closest approach 23 m). Rounding wider costs less than it looks:

| rounding radius | time | avg mph | swings wide by | peak lat g |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 m | 166.3 s | 43.3 | 34 m | 1.35 |
| 40 m | 159.4 s | 45.2 | 21 m | 1.35 |
| 60 m | 153.0 s | 47.1 | 15 m | 1.29 |
| 80 m | 150.9 s | 47.7 | 13 m | 1.16 |

**Plan the roundings at 60–80 m for a 50 mph boat.** It is 13–15 s faster than
25 m, because the boat holds speed through the turn, and the lateral load is
lower.

**Error does not accumulate over laps.** Lap RMS cross-track 14.0 / 14.4 /
14.3 m for the three complete laps (it is dominated by the roundings). There is
no integrator drift or bias creep.

**With the prop as logged** the boat tops out at 28.8 mph loaded and averages
26.2 mph (275 s) when commanded 50.

## 4e. PEP26 Autonomy rules — what is actually specified

Source: [PEP26 Autonomy Division rules
(PDF)](https://www.navalengineers.org/Portals/16/PEP/2026/Signup/PEP_Rules2025-2026_Autonomy.pdf),
ASNE/ONR. Event 14–16 April 2026, Portsmouth City Park, Portsmouth VA
(Elizabeth River). **Re-download the PEP27 rules before relying on any of
this.**

### Compliance check against the current design

| rule | limit | Osprey | |
|---|---|---|---|
| battery total voltage | ≤ 55.5 V | 12S: 44.4 V nominal, 50.4 V full | **OK** |
| battery capacity | < 500 Ah | n/a | OK |
| payload | 30 lb removable, team-supplied | 13.6 kg in `Vessel.m_payload` | **OK** |
| race distance | 2 miles (3219 m) | 166 s at a 50 mph cruise | **OK** |
| heat window | 55 min | uses **5%** of it | **OK** |

### The scoring changes the design priorities

- **10 points per completed half-mile** → 40 points for the full distance
- **20 / 16 / 12 / 8 / 4** points for 1st through 5th
- Race performance is 60 of 100 total (white paper 20, video 20)

**Completing the distance is worth twice as much as winning it.** The required
average speed to finish inside the heat is **0.98 m/s (2.2 mph)**. That is the
argument for every conservative choice in the autopilot — clamping the rudder
at ventilation onset, capping yaw rate inside the roll envelope — and it is the
standing question over the 50 mph decision (§4b).

### Rule 21

> *"Autonomous: Teams must demonstrate how the kill switch is engaged when
> navigation information (like GPS data) is missing or corrupted."*

The MATLAB autopilot rode out a GPS dropout by dead-reckoning on the IMU. **Under
the rules that is the wrong behaviour.** The Lua autopilot disarms 500 ms after
the fix is lost (§4c).

### Other verified requirements bearing on the build

- Main power disconnect through which **all** propulsion current passes (Rule 7)
- Fuse in the battery circuit for 24–55 V systems (Rule 9)
- **Positive buoyancy — must float fully flooded** (Rule 19)
- **Towing bridle across both hulls** for catamarans (Rule 13)
- Boat-hook-reachable restraint structure for uncrewed craft (Rule 14)
- 5-minute launch, ramp to open water, payload weighed dockside (Rule 11)
- No pre-built motors or kits
- **Demo video gate: 200 m or 2 min of open-water operation with a documented
  thrust measurement** — required to get a race slot

### What the rules do NOT specify

Course shape and waypoint coordinates; number, spacing and size of marks;
turn angles and radii; waypoint arrival tolerance; number of laps; standoff
distances; on-water speed limit; any station-keeping or loitering task. The
two-mark oval, 0.25 mile apart, comes from the team.

## 5. Validity limits — where this model stops being usable

**Cavitation.** σ falls below 0.5 at **19.9 m/s (44 mph)**, is 0.93 at 50 mph
and 0.154 at the 35.8 m/s design speed (independently reproducing the thesis's
≈0.15). **The attached-flow lift model is void above ~20 m/s, and the race
cruise is above it.** Every 50 mph rudder number is an extrapolation, and
optimistic on authority.

**Ventilation.** Control authority is **non-monotonic in deflection**: for the
old 75 mm blade at 13.4 m/s the yaw moment peaks at 10° (64.4 N·m) and falls to
38.7 N·m at 12°, latched until the blade unloads below 6°. The autopilot
therefore clamps at ventilation onset, not at the 35° mechanical stop.

**Speed sag.** Open-loop turns at 10° lose **21–35%** of forward speed between
10 and 50 mph. Gains schedule on measured speed, so the controller chases a
moving operating point through every hard turn.

**Munk moment.** At 5° sideslip it is **40% of full useful rudder** for the new
blade at nominal added mass, and **150%** at the high-sway-added-mass edge of
the range — **with its sign undetermined**. (Old blade: 108% and 409%.) In a
settled 10° turn above 30 mph it is 1.6× the rudder moment. This is the
mechanism behind the 50 mph losses in §4b.

**Roll envelope — now nearly binding.** Inner-sponson unloading at 2.49 g
(`h_cg` assumed 0.12 m); quasi-static safe limit 1.50 g with a 0.6 safety
factor. The race peaks at **1.35 g** in the roundings — 90% of the safe limit —
and only the autopilot's yaw-rate cap keeps it there: an open-loop 10° turn at
50 mph reaches 2.45 g. **The model is 3-DOF and cannot see roll or hooking**;
this check is all there is. A CG 20% higher than assumed puts the roundings
past the limit. Measure `h_cg`.

## 6. Powertrain

| speed | hull resistance (loaded) | power into water | thrust available: prop as logged | sized for 55 mph (assumed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 mph | 189 N | 2.5 kW | 173 N | 583 N |
| 40 mph | 299 N | 5.4 kW | 144 N | 483 N |
| **50 mph** | **336 N** | **7.5 kW** | **114 N** | **384 N** |
| 55 mph | 334 N | 8.2 kW | 99 N | 334 N |
| 80 mph | 281 N | 10.1 kW | 25 N | 85 N |

(Resistance falls past ~50 mph as the tunnel's air lift takes weight off the
pads.) At the 63% chain efficiency implied by the team's own log, 50 mph needs
~12 kW electrical, **~135 A per motor** against the 45 A logged; 80 mph needs
~16 kW, ~180 A per motor. These do not depend on the prop — only on the hull
resistance model, which validated to ~3% against the logged 13.4 m/s run
(2324 W against ~2400 W).

**The prop as logged is the limit, not the electronics:** it tops out at
28.8 mph loaded. The ESC upgrade and a prop matched to 50 mph are what the
race plan assumes.

## 7. The minimum on-water test set

Ranked by how much uncertainty each collapses per hour of boat time.

| # | Test | Instrumentation | Duration | Identifies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Drift/sideslip run**: hold rudder, let the boat settle, measure crab angle | GPS COG vs IMU heading | 1 h | **Y_v̇ ⇒ the Munk moment magnitude AND sign** (A12) — the mechanism behind the 50 mph losses |
| 2 | **Zig-zag (20°/20°) at 3 speeds** (5, 8, 12 m/s) | IMU @100 Hz, GPS @5 Hz, rudder feedback pot | 2 h | **K′ and T′** directly, and their speed scaling |
| 3 | **Bifilar pendulum swing** (on the bench, not the water) | stopwatch, two parallel lines | 1 h | **Iz** to ~5%. Kills the ±50% sweep |
| 4 | **Steady turn circles**, 5°/10°/15°/20° rudder, both directions, 2 speeds | same + speed log | 2 h | **Ventilation onset and depth of loss** (A5), and the non-monotonic authority |
| 5 | **Pull test on the tiller** at 3 speeds, straight-line | inline load cell on the steering cable | 1 h | **Hinge moment and the stock position** (A10, A9) — settles the servo spec |
| 6 | **Coast-down from top speed** | GPS @10 Hz | 0.5 h | Hull resistance curve beyond the single 13.4 m/s point |

Tests 1–3 decide whether 50 mph is controllable; test 5 decides the servo.

## 8. Assumptions ranked by impact

From the Monte Carlo rank correlations and the lost-draw parameters (§4b),
plus the servo and roll sensitivities.

1. **A11 hull derivatives `Nv`, `Nr`, `Yv` (±5×)** — the strongest correlates of
   losing the boat at 50 mph. *Zig-zag and turn circles.*
2. **A12 sway added mass `Y_v̇` → the Munk moment's size and sign** — decides
   whether the boat is directionally divergent. *Sideslip run.*
3. **A10 stock position / A9 tiller arm** — decide whether the servo works at
   all with the new blade. *Callipers.*
4. **A14 CG height** — the roundings use 90% of the roll margin at 50 mph.
5. **A1 yaw inertia** — lost draws sit at 1.17× nominal. *Bifilar pendulum.*
6. **A5 ventilation onset and depth** — bounds the useful rudder.
7. **A6 cavitation ceiling** — the whole race is past it; optimistic on rudder
   authority.
8. **A18 propulsion** — sets top speed and makes the launch harsher than a real
   one; interacts with the launch spin-outs.

## 9. Corrections to earlier versions of this summary

Kept in the record, because each one changed a conclusion.

1. **"The thesis's 66 kg·cm is conservative by 46%" — wrong.** The MATLAB
   servo sizing evaluated one point, 35° ventilated, which is not the peak.
   Correct: 64.6 kg·cm clamped, 82.7 full travel (§3).
2. **Validation power 2534 W — wrong mass.** The logged run carried no payload;
   the MATLAB check used the loaded mass. Correct: 2324 W against ~2400 W.
3. **MATLAB thrust 9% high.** The prop calibration read the loaded-mass hull
   table. Every MATLAB result after the table was added had 9% too much thrust.
4. **MATLAB compute delay was zero**, not the one tick it documented. Its
   controller results were slightly optimistic on phase margin.
5. **"The as-built rudder completes the real course in every condition"** —
   true at 8 m/s, nominal parameters. At 50 mph under uncertainty it loses most
   boats (§4b).
6. **"Roll envelope not binding, 15–36% of the safe yaw rate"** — true at
   8 m/s. At 50 mph the roundings use 90% of it (§5).
7. **An intermediate run of the Python Monte Carlo** capped every race at 400 s
   and counted a timeout as "lost", which made a 20 mph cruise look worse than
   50 mph. The limit now scales with speed and timeouts are reported
   separately.
8. **The "empty sizing band"** (MATLAB §2) came from a turn-radius spec invented
   before the course was known. The real course never needed that turn rate;
   the band was an artefact of the placeholder spec.

Items 1–4 are MATLAB model bugs found by the Python port; details in
`../python/PORTING.md`. The MATLAB model is frozen and still has them.
